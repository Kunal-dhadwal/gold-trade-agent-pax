# ADR-004: AI vs. Deterministic System Boundaries & Veto Authority
**Status:** Approved & Frozen  
**Date:** 2026-10-08  
**Author:** Lead Quantitative Systems Engineer  
**Governing Documents:** `PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`

---

## 1. CONTEXT & PROBLEM STATEMENT

Large Language Models (LLMs) and generative AI agents excel at synthesizing complex market contexts, generating novel trading hypotheses, diagnosing strategy failure modes, and translating strategy logic into code (e.g., Pine Script).

However, LLMs possess fundamental characteristics that make them unsuitable for direct execution authority in institutional financial trading:
1. **Non-determinism:** Identical inputs may yield different outputs.
2. **Hallucination Risk:** Models may fabricate price levels, indicators, or risk metrics.
3. **Latency Inconsistency:** API round-trips and inference latency are variable and unsuitable for real-time market execution.
4. **Prompt Injection & Security Vulnerabilities:** Unsanitized external inputs could compromise model behavior.

Without strict architectural isolation, an AI component could inadvertently bypass risk limits, execute unauthorized orders, or hallucinate position sizes.

---

## 2. DECISION

We establish rigid boundaries between AI components and deterministic quantitative/execution systems across all phases of the project:

### A. Phase Isolation
- **Phase 1: Zero AI Dependency.** All indicators, market regimes, backtesting calculations, and metrics must be 100% deterministic Python/NumPy code.
- **Phase 2: AI Confined to Strategy Factory.** AI agents (Market Analyst, Strategy Researcher, Performance Evaluator) operate strictly in the research and hypothesis generation domain.

### B. LLM Provider Abstraction
- All AI capabilities must sit behind an abstract `LLMProvider` interface.
- **Primary Provider:** Local / Self-hosted LLM (via Ollama or vLLM) for zero-cost, private research.
- **Secondary Provider:** Google Gemini API (free tier / developer API) as an alternative backend.
- Proprietary provider lock-in is strictly forbidden.

### C. The Deterministic Pipeline & Veto Precedence
Under no circumstances may an AI agent communicate directly with an exchange API or execution pipeline. The system enforces the invariant:
$$\mathbf{AI\ Agent} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Strategy\ Signal} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Deterministic\ Risk\ Engine} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Execution\ Engine} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Exchange}$$

1. **Unconditional Veto:** The Deterministic Risk Engine evaluates 8 hardcoded mathematical rules (max daily drawdown, position size cap, spread threshold, max loss streak, kill switch status). It has unconditional authority to drop or abort any signal generated by an AI or strategy.
2. **Zero Key Exposure:** AI agents and prompts must never have access to exchange API keys, secrets, passphrase credentials, or database connection strings.
3. **Execution Gate:** No AI-generated strategy can ever trade live until it has passed deterministic backtesting, walk-forward analysis, Monte Carlo simulation, out-of-sample holdout verification, and a 4-week / 50-trade paper trading trial.

---

## 3. CONSEQUENCES

### Positive:
- **Absolute Risk Containment:** The platform is physically incapable of catastrophic runaway execution caused by an AI hallucination or prompt injection.
- **Auditability:** Every strategy hypothesis produced by an AI is converted into deterministic code and logged into `agent_decisions` for forensic review.
- **Reproducibility:** Research and backtesting results remain 100% reproducible regardless of future LLM model deprecations or weight changes.

### Negative / Trade-offs:
- Adds structured abstraction layers between AI generation and backtesting execution.
- AI cannot dynamically adapt trade orders mid-bar without passing through deterministic risk evaluation.

---

## 4. COMPLIANCE & VERIFICATION

1. In Phase 1 codebase, no imports of LLM SDKs (e.g. `google.genai`, `openai`, `langchain`) may exist in active dependencies.
2. In Phase 2, all AI calls must route through `src/ai/provider.py`.
3. Architectural reviews must verify that no code path exists from `src/ai/` to `src/execution/` or exchange credential stores.
